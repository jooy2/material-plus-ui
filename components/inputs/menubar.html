MPMenubar ​

The strip of words at the top of an application — File, Edit, View — each of which opens a menu.

ReactFlutter
tsx
import { MPMenubar, MPMenubarMenu, MPMenuItem } from 'material-plus-ui';

<MPMenubar>
  <MPMenubarMenu label="File">
    <MPMenuItem shortcut="Mod+N" onClick={create}>
      New
    </MPMenuItem>
  </MPMenubarMenu>
</MPMenubar>;
dart
import 'package:material_plus_ui/material_plus_ui.dart';

MPMenubar(
  menus: <MPMenubarMenu>[
    MPMenubarMenu(
      label: 'File',
      items: <MPMenuEntry>[
        MPMenuItem(label: 'New', shortcut: 'Mod+N', onPressed: create),
      ],
    ),
  ],
);

What makes it a bar rather than a row of menus ​

What happens once one of them is open.

Moving along the strip walks through the others instead of closing the one you left, and the arrow keys move between the menus as well as inside them. Three separate MPMenus side by side do neither: crossing from one to the next closes the first and opens nothing.

Base UI owns all of that, along with the menubar role — which is what tells a screen reader the strip is one widget with one tab stop rather than six unrelated buttons.

The bar owns it here: only one of its menus is open at a time, a word the pointer comes onto opens its menu in place of the open one, and an arrow along the strip that the open menu has no submenu for opens the next menu with its first row lit. The bar is a node with the menu bar role, which is what tells a screen reader it is one widget with one tab stop rather than six unrelated buttons.

The words are not buttons ​

A menu bar's rows are drawn a rung below the control ladder, and the reason is what a menu bar sits on.

It is almost always inside something that already has a height of its own: an MPHeader, a title bar, a toolbar. Drawn at control height, File Edit View would be three buttons in a row and the strip would be taller than the thing it is on.

They take MD3's label-large — the same role an MPMenu's own rows take — because the word on the bar and the rows behind it are one object. Setting the strip in a control's title-medium makes the bar read as a row of buttons that happen to open lists.

Why it draws no surface ​

Same reason. A sheet under a strip that is already on a sheet is two sheets, and the second one has nothing to say — the refusal MPContainer makes, one level down.

Put it on something:

tsx
<MPHeader brand="Acme" size="sm">
  <MPMenubar size="sm">…</MPMenubar>
</MPHeader>
dart
MPHeader(
  brand: const <Widget>[Text('Acme')],
  size: MPSize.sm,
  child: MPMenubar(size: MPSize.sm, menus: menus),
)

Which word is open ​

Colour, and nothing else: the open word takes the accent and the state layer stays lit under it.

The word does not move and the strip does not change height, which matters more here than almost anywhere: a reader crossing the bar is pointing at a target that must not shift out from under them, and every word on the strip is a target.

One menu on the bar: the word, and the rows behind it.

It has no size and no color of its own — both belong to the bar, which is the only place they can be set once and hold for every menu on it. The rows inside are the same MPMenuItem, MPMenuSeparator, MPMenuGroup, MPMenuCheckboxItem, MPMenuRadioItem and MPMenuSubmenu an MPMenu takes, because it is the same menu.

tsx
<MPMenubarMenu label="View">
  <MPMenuCheckboxItem checked={rulers} onCheckedChange={setRulers}>
    Rulers
  </MPMenuCheckboxItem>
  <MPMenuSubmenu label="Zoom">
    <MPMenuItem onClick={() => zoom(2)}>200%</MPMenuItem>
  </MPMenuSubmenu>
</MPMenubarMenu>
dart
MPMenubarMenu(
  label: 'View',
  items: <MPMenuEntry>[
    MPMenuCheckboxItem(
      label: 'Rulers',
      checked: rulers,
      onChanged: (bool next) => setState(() => rulers = next),
    ),
    MPMenuSubmenu(
      label: 'Zoom',
      items: <MPMenuEntry>[MPMenuItem(label: '200%', onPressed: () => zoom(2))],
    ),
  ],
)

When this is the wrong component ​

When there are more actions than a strip can hold.

A menu bar's whole advantage is that every heading is visible before you go looking for one, and a bar of twelve words has already lost it. That is an MPCommandPalette: the reader types what they want instead of remembering which heading it was filed under.

The two live together well. A palette bound to Mod+K and a bar of five words is a product that is fast for the reader who knows it and legible to the one who does not.

Accessibility ​

  • The strip is a menubar with one tab stop. Tab moves past the whole bar; the arrow keys move along it.
  • Each word is a menuitem with aria-haspopup and aria-expanded, and its menu is owned by it.
  • The strip is a node with the menu bar role and one tab stop. Tab moves past the whole bar, and the arrow keys along it move between the words and wrap at the ends unless loopFocus is off. Home and End go to either end. Under directional navigation the arrows halt at the ends and an arrow past one goes on to the next control.
  • On a row the down arrow opens a word's menu with its first row lit; on a column it is the arrow pointing across. While a menu is open, the arrow along the bar that the menu has no submenu for opens the next menu instead.
  • Each word is a node with the menu item role that says whether its menu is expanded. Once a menu closes the focus goes back to its word.
  • A disabled word stays on the strip at the spec's 38% and opens nothing.
  • The focus ring is drawn inside the word — the one place in this library it is inset. A strip of words has no gaps to draw a ring in, so an outside ring would be painted under whichever word comes after it.
